What the research actually says

8 s

The “goldfish attention span” has no study behind it

In 2015 headlines claimed our attention span had dropped to 8 seconds, below a goldfish's 9. The BBC traced the number and found no research behind it. Researchers it spoke to called an “average attention span” meaningless, because attention depends on the task.

BBC News: Busting the attention span myth · Time, 2015
47 s

The scary number measures switching screens, not watching

Gloria Mark found that people switch between screens about every 47 seconds, against 2.5 minutes in 2004. That is about tabs and notifications at work. It says nothing about whether you can follow a good video.

University of California, 2024
2012

YouTube started paying for minutes

In 2012 YouTube made watch time the main signal for recommendations. Since 2020 a video needs only 8 minutes to carry ads in the middle. The system rewards minutes watched, not what you learned in them.

YouTube Creator Blog, 2012 · Tubefilter, 2020
85%

People want denser videos, not shorter ones

When viewers change the playback speed, they speed up more than 85% of the time. Viewers save over 900 years of watching every day that way. That's not a short attention span. That's people skipping the water.

YouTube Official Blog, 2022
21%

AI slop is already in the feed

Kapwing opened a fresh account and checked the first 500 Shorts it was shown. 104 of them, about one in five, were AI-generated. In 2025 YouTube renamed its rule on “repetitive or mass-produced” videos to “inauthentic content”.

Kapwing, 2025 · YouTube Partner Program policies
52%

Half of popular ADHD TikToks were misleading

A 2022 study looked at the 100 most popular TikTok videos about ADHD. 52 were misleading, and only 21 were useful. A feed full of padded videos and a feed full of self-diagnosis feed each other.

Canadian Journal of Psychiatry, 2022

ADHD is real, and it deserves a real assessment by a specialist. This page is not about that. It's about the many people who were told their brain is broken when the video was simply bad.

Every click is a trade. Your time for their watch time.

More and more people decide they can't focus because a twenty-minute video felt endless. Often the problem isn't the viewer. It's a video stretched to hit a length, with three minutes of substance and seventeen of intro, recap and "before we start".

Here's the one that drives me mad. You look for a review of a game because you want an opinion: how the combat feels, which mechanics are clever, whether it's worth your weekend. You open a twenty-minute “review” and get the author's own playthrough, cut up and narrated: here you go left, here you open a chest, here the boss has a second phase. That isn't a review. It's a retelling, made long because long pays.

Long isn't the enemy. A thirty-minute talk from a game developer on how they built their animation system can be worth every second. So can a good lecture. What wastes time is padding, sold with a shocked face on the thumbnail and a title that promises what the video never delivers. That's a dark pattern, and it works on everyone.

Choosing a video means giving a creator part of your life. BetterYouTube helps you give it to people who respect it.

Time is the most valuable thing I have. Respect should go both ways.

What it does

Dims videos almost nobody watched

Home, the “Up next” column and, if you like, search results. You choose the line: 50K views by default, anywhere from 1K to 5M.

Spots tiny channels and content farms

Cards don't show subscriber counts, so the extension asks YouTube once per channel and remembers it for a week. Channels you subscribe to are never touched.

Skips Shorts nobody liked

Removes Shorts shelves from Home. In the Shorts player, a Short with fewer likes than you set is covered, muted and paused, so you scroll past it without hearing a second.

Reads the video so you don't have to

Open it on any video and get the point, written from the transcript. Not sure it covers what you need? Ask it. It answers from what was actually said, not from the thumbnail.

Bring your own AI

Chrome's built-in AI works out of the box, free and offline. Already pay for Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI? Put them to work. Or paste a key from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or OpenRouter, or point it at your own Ollama.

Your rules, in your language

Dim or hide, pick the pages, set every threshold, pause with one switch. Light or dark and the language of your browser, with more translations on the way.

An honest caveat: views are a rough signal. A great new video starts at zero views too. That's why the default is to dim, not to hide, and hovering a dimmed video brings it back.

Know what's inside before you press play

Twenty minutes of video. Three minutes of substance. Find out which part is which in five seconds. Open the extension on any video and it hands you the point, then answers whatever you ask about it. No more watching to the end to learn there was no end.

Nothing goes to us

No serversThe extension has no backend. Chrome's built-in AI and your coding agents run on your computer. A cloud AI you pick gets the transcript with your own key, and nobody else does.
No trackingNo analytics, no accounts, no ads. Your settings sync through your own browser profile.
One site onlyIt runs on youtube.com and nowhere else, with a single “storage” permission for your settings.
